📊 Berlin Mietspiegel

1. Project Overview

What is the Berlin Mietspiegel?

A Mietspiegel (rent index) is an official document published by the Berlin Senate that establishes the legally admissible rent levels for residential properties. It categorizes rent by:

  • Wohnlage — location quality (einfach/simple, mittel/medium, gut/good)
  • Baujahr — construction year period (from pre-1918 to post-2011)
  • Wohnfläche — apartment size in m²

The Berlin Mietspiegel is a "qualifizierter Mietspiegel" — the highest legal grade, admissible in court without further evidence. It directly affects rent control (Mietpreisbremse), tenant disputes, and housing policy.

Why Three Data Sources?

No single source tells the full story of Berlin rents. By combining three independent datasets, we can reveal the gap between what the law says and what the market actually charges:

The census rent represents what current tenants actually pay — including long-term tenants with older, cheaper contracts. This is why the census average (€7.97/m²) is significantly lower than the market average (€10.79/m²): new listings cost dramatically more than existing contracts.

3. What You See on the Map

Heatmap

The heatmap shows either Immoscout24 market rents or Zensus 2022 census rents (toggleable). Colors are z-score normalized relative to the Berlin average, not national:

  • ● Green — below Berlin average (cheaper areas)
  • ● Yellow — at Berlin average
  • ● Red — above Berlin average (expensive areas)

District Labels

Berlin's 12 Bezirke are shown as text labels with their estimated average rent per m². The estimates are derived from the Berlin Senate's Wohnlage classification (400,505 address-level points) cross-referenced with the official Mietspiegel rent tables.

Click Anywhere

Clicking the map performs nearest-neighbor lookups across all three data layers:

  • Market rent — nearest Immoscout24 grid cell with €/m², year, and PLZ
  • Census rent — nearest Zensus 2022 cell showing what residents actually pay
  • Official Mietspiegel — Berlin's legal reference rent, with the gap to market

The tooltip explains: "New listings cost X% more than what current tenants pay" — plain language, no z-score jargon.

Berlin rents doubled in 10 years. The 2021→2023 period alone saw +18.4% growth — the steepest increase in a single edition cycle. This coincides with the Mietendeckel (rent freeze) being struck down by Germany's Constitutional Court in April 2021.
